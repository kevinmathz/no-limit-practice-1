## Method

- Abstract game per cell (n players, equal stacks S bb, blinds 0.5/1, chip EV in bb): 3-card deal -> simultaneous choose (1755 classes x 6 actions) -> push/fold with all reveals public.
- Opener infoset (pos, kept169, shown_is_low, max revealed rank behind); caller infoset (pos, kept169, shown_is_low, jammer's revealed rank). BB never opens (walk).
- Mini-batch MCCFR with DCFR discounting (alpha 1.5, beta 0, gamma 2). Per iteration: B_bet sampled deals with choose actions sampled from the current strategy; the push/fold tree is evaluated in exact expectation (every jammer x every caller subset) giving counterfactual values for all seats' betting infosets; on a B_ch sub-batch every seat's 6 choose actions are evaluated with the others fixed (external sampling of the choose layer).
- Showdown: hu_eq heads-up, normalised product of pairwise equities multiway (no card removal between hands).

## Convergence (bb per hand per seat)

- `BR pf` = gain of a best-responding seat that changes only its push/fold (choose fixed), averaged over seats; max seat in brackets. Cross-validated (BR picked on one sample, measured on another) so sampling noise does not inflate it; in-sample (upper-biased) value after the slash.
- `BR choose` = gain of a seat best-responding in the choose layer only (its push/fold fixed), CV / in-sample.
- `loss lowR` = EV a single seat loses by always revealing the LOWER of its two kept cards (same hand selection as equilibrium), vs equilibrium opponents who keep their strategy; `lowR expl` = same but every opponent best-responds in push/fold to that leak. `hiR` = always reveal the higher card.
- `loss dflt` = loss from the fallback policy (best-equity hand, 50/50 reveal).

## Run summary

- Full run: 49 cells (players 2..8 x stacks 3,5,8,12,17,25,40 bb), 6 worker processes, 11.0 min wall-clock on a loaded
  machine (load ~16 on 8 cores), then 1.6 min to re-solve 4 cells that had been starved of CPU (n=4 S=3/5/8, n=8 S=40).
- Solve budget: ~44 s per cell for n<=3, ~68 s for n=4..5, ~84 s for n>=6. Iterations (batches): n=2 93-114, n=3 55-88,
  n=4 77-134, n=5 53-82, n=6 55-79, n=7 36-48, n=8 29-45 (see `iters` in the npz). Batch = 40k deals (n=2) down to 3k
  deals (n=8) for betting; the choose sub-batch is 4k down to 250 deals (each seat x 6 actions).
- Each cell is saved to `cells/n{n}_S{S}.npz` when it finishes; `cfr_tables.npz` is packed from those files.
  `--resume` skips cells already saved and `--report-from-raw` rebuilds this report.
- Best-response diagnostics were run only for n in {2,6} x S in {8,40}. The quick tables (`cfr_tables_quick.npz`)
  used 40 s per cell (n=7/8: about 10-12 iterations only).
- Unreached push/fold infosets (`visits_* == 0`, e.g. BB opening) default to 0.5. Choose classes that were never
  sampled default to keeping the best-equity hand with a 50/50 reveal.

## Findings

- **Convergence.** In HU the push/fold strategy is essentially unexploitable within sampling noise: the
  cross-validated BR gain is about 0 and the in-sample upper bound is 0.01-0.03 bb/hand. The choose layer is within
  0.002-0.006 bb/hand (CV). At 6-max, the CV push/fold BR gain is 0.004 bb/hand per seat (max seat 0.008-0.025) and
  the choose BR gain is 0.01-0.03 bb/hand. The in-sample upper bounds (0.04-0.18) mostly reflect noise from
  ~100k samples spread over about 9k infosets per seat. n=7/8 got fewer iterations and are the least converged
  cells; treat their exact mixing frequencies as noisy.
- **Leaking the reveal costs real EV.** HU 8bb: always revealing the lower card loses 0.050 bb/hand against
  equilibrium opponents who do not adapt, and **0.145 bb/hand** when they best-respond. At 40bb it loses
  0.167 / 0.270 bb/hand. Always revealing the higher card is cheaper (0.026 / 0.092 at 8bb, ~0 at 40bb), because the
  equilibrium mostly does that anyway. At 6-max the leak costs about 0.06-0.11 bb/hand.
  The "always reveal higher" rows can come out negative at 6-max. That is noise plus the multiway approximation:
  in this abstraction, revealing high is close to equilibrium there.
- **Aces.** With exactly one Ace in the kept non-pair hand, the Ace is revealed only 17-31% of the time in HU at
  3-12bb, but about 50% at 25-40bb and 50-65% at 4-8 players. Short HU hides the Ace, since the hidden Ace is what
  wins all-ins and showing it would deter calls. Multiway it is shown about half the time.
- **Revealed Ace means AA?** P(AA | revealed A) is 0.23-0.29 HU short-stacked, 0.12 HU deep, 0.10-0.15 at 4-8
  players. A revealed Ace is mostly Ax offsuit/suited; the equilibrium does not let it signal AA.
- **Pairs.** HU short: dealt a pair plus kicker, the pair is kept 90-97% of the time. That falls with depth and
  table size, to 55-65% at 6-8 players. Small pairs are often broken for two high cards, e.g. P(keep 22 | 22x) is
  0.2-0.3 at 6-max. A revealed deuce in HU 3-8bb is a pair 50-80% of the time: the low card is usually shown only
  when it is half of a pair. Deep or multiway, P(pair | revealed r) is flat at about 0.07-0.15 across ranks.
- **Mixing.** 40-75% of the deal mass has a genuinely mixed reveal (minority action >10%). It rises with the
  number of players. Non-pair hands reveal the lower card 22-55% of the time: more often when short and HU, less
  when deep HU (about 23%), and back up to 40-50% at 7-8 players.
